# Lore: draft

> Scratch version, 3 Nov 2026. Something to chew on, not canon. Nothing here is decided.

## The Flood

The world was already at war long before the water came. The question at the heart of it was always the same: which God is real? The war ran so long that the weapons became relics and the armies became churches. That's why the arsenal feels forged from a thousand generations of fighting: it was.

Then God sent the Flood. In Genesis the flood comes because the earth was filled with violence, and here that's the point: the war is *why* it rained.

The game is set **during** the Flood, not after it. The water is still rising.

The survivors all agree God sent it. They can't agree on who or what that God is, and that's what turned the old factions into zealots. The ones who survived are, almost by definition, the most militarised people left: whoever was inside the fortresses, bunkers and war-citadels when the water came. The Flood didn't end the war. It shrank the battlefield to one tower.

Possible hook: God promised never to flood the world again. Then it rained. Either God lied, or this isn't the same God. That broken promise is the schism.

## The spire: a new Babel

The spire is the tallest structure left above the water. Everyone is climbing it, the new Tower of Babel.

At Babel, God confused the builders' languages so they couldn't work together. Here, every faction is climbing the same tower toward the same God and still can't agree on who that God is. That's why they're shooting at each other on the stairs instead of climbing side by side.

Babel irony: the first tower was built by people trying to reach God by their own hands. This time they're climbing with manufactured men.

## The vat-arks (why clones)

There are no armies left; the population is a rounding error. But the old war industry had vats, because after a thousand generations of fighting nobody waits for soldiers to be born. Each faction's ark isn't a boat full of animals. It's a gene vault and a decanting chamber, saved from the water. They didn't preserve people; they preserved the pattern of soldiers.

"A thousand generations" is literal: those are clone generations.

Why send them up:

- **The water is still rising.** The vats sit at the base of the spire. If no one reaches the top before the sea takes the vats, the faction ends. That's the urgency behind every run.
- **The Definitor only answers whoever reaches it.** You can't send the whole faction, because there's no whole faction left. You send four.
- **To the zealots, cloning is resurrection.** A dead trooper is re-decanted; the faithful literally rise again. Permadeath still stings: the person is gone even if their copy walks out of the vat.
- **Meta-progression is relic logic.** What a dead squad learned comes back down in their blood: recovered genetic material, mutations from the climb. The next batch inherits it. Gene samples treated like saints' bones.

Fallback if cloning feels bolted on: lineage. Recruits are children or devotees of the fallen, carrying a relic instead of genes.

## Four factions, four reasons to climb

Each one answers "who sent the Flood?" differently, and their goals at the top are mutually exclusive.

**Halo Guard (gold): "God sent it, and God was right."**
The loyalists. The Flood was just; humanity earned it. They climb to stand before the throne and be counted among the saved, and to stop anyone unworthy from getting there. To them, everyone else on the stairs is the reason it rained.

**Neon Choir (cyan): "God is still speaking, and we can't hear it."**
Babel's curse was the confusion of tongues. The Choir believe the original voice of God is still broadcasting and the static is the punishment. The higher you climb, the cleaner the signal. They want to hear the one true word and sing it back to the drowned world. God in the transmission.

**Ash Legion (magenta): "God is whoever wins."**
The old war's army, still fighting. Ash is what's left after fire. The Flood was a culling, the strong survived, and the throne at the top goes to whoever takes it. They aren't climbing to be judged; they're climbing to become the answer.

**The Hollow (green): "The Flood didn't go far enough."**
They want to see the world consumed. God didn't punish the world; God started ending it and then stopped. The spire is the last thing above the waterline, the last thing between the world and being fully consumed, so the Hollow climb to finish the job. The others want something *from* the top. The Hollow want there to be no top. Their demon names fit: the fallen as patrons of the end itself.

(Spare idea: "God is the enemy" is still free for a fifth faction or a splinter group.)

## The Definitor

It's the one who *defines*. It sits at the top and settles the question everyone is killing each other over. Each faction reads it differently:

- Halo Guard: the judge
- Neon Choir: the voice
- Ash Legion: a throne to seize
- The Hollow: the keystone to pull so everything comes down

Never say which is right.

## How it could show up in play

- **Rising water is sudden death.** Worms already has it. Here it's the Flood catching up. Long battles end because the sea takes the floor, which also helps with battle length.
- **Levels need to read as ascent.** Each stage should show it: a higher floor, the waterline lower beneath, the drowned city shrinking. Vertical tower stages also bring back the up-and-around movement the drowned city map is missing.
- **Faction play styles.** The Hollow lean into destruction (cratering, collapsing floors, terrain splash). Halo Guard lean defensive. Neon Choir lean on comms, drones and support. Ash Legion lean straight aggression.
- **Different endings.** Reaching the top could resolve differently by faction: judgement, revelation, conquest or collapse.
- **Friend-mode maps stay lore-free.** The Flood and Babel belong to the roguelike run.

## Open questions

- After or during? Leaning during. Note the title screen currently says the flood is gone ("The flood is gone. This is what it left."), which reads as after. If we go with during, that line changes.
- Is the Flood literally the biblical one, or a second Flood in a high-tech world? A second Flood lets the old faiths, scripture and tech all survive with nobody agreeing what any of it means.
- Do clones have souls? Each faction probably answers differently.
- Does the name change follow from this? "Sermon of Shells" quietly fits the drowned world.
